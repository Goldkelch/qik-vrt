On a long journey of consciousness, the search for causality, and the light I have left on for others

There are journeys for which you board no vehicle, buy no ticket, and cross no national border. No one can see directly when they begin. No one stands by the wayside and can confirm how far you have already travelled. And yet such journeys can take a person further from their original starting point than any movement through geographical space.

My journey was a journey of consciousness.

It took me through mathematics, computer science, physics, technology, language, causality, epistemology, and the emotional borderlands that arise when one person spends a long time trying not merely to think a far-reaching idea, but to bring it into a coherent, verifiable form that can be communicated.

This text is therefore intended to be more than a description of my work. It is meant to record, for myself and for the people around me, that I have returned from this journey.

Not unchanged. Not without effort. Not without traces.

But returned.

And I have brought something back.

⸻

The catalyst

At the beginning there was no finished worldview. Nor was there a completed theory. Instead, there was a catalyst: encountering representations of mathematical fibrations, and the possibility they opened up of looking at space, time, and connectedness differently from the way I had before.

A fibration is, first of all, a mathematical structural principle. It allows a space to be considered not merely as a single, undifferentiated whole, but as an ordered connection between different levels: a base and the fibres structured over it.

For me, this idea was not merely a mathematical detail. It opened up a space for thought.

Suddenly, it seemed at least possible that the reality we experience as spacetime might also be organised differently from what our everyday intuition suggests. Not necessarily as a simple stage on which events take place one after another, but as a fabric of relationships, bindings, transitions, perspectives, and causally connected states.

This thought was not yet knowledge in the strict scientific sense. It was an opening. A question. An invitation to go further.

I followed that invitation.

⸻

The decision not to look away

Many unusual thoughts disappear again. They are discarded, forgotten, or displaced by the demands of everyday life. Some thoughts really are wrong turns. Others are interesting possibilities that lead nowhere.

But occasionally you encounter a question that does not go away simply because you ignore it.

My response was not to insist on being right straight away. It was to take the question seriously.

What if a deeper structural connection lay behind the different phenomena, concepts, and disciplines?

What if certain contradictions seemed irresolvable only because we were considering them within an unsuitable representation?

What if causality must not be equated with temporal sequence?

What if results, effects, and observations needed to be distinguished more carefully from one another?

What if a system could determine its own state not through assertion alone, but only through reobservation bound to that state?

Such questions do not remain abstract when you follow them consistently. They reach into your own perception. They change which relationships you recognise, which contradictions you can no longer overlook, and which explanations no longer suffice.

I decided not to look away.

⸻

A journey undertaken alone

I undertook this journey largely alone.

This deserves emphasis, not to belittle other people and not to place myself outside the human community. It deserves emphasis because solitary work of this kind brings a particular burden and responsibility.

In an established research setting, tasks are normally shared among many people. There are colleagues, specialist groups, reviewers, institutional procedures, shared conceptual frameworks, and common infrastructure. Hypotheses can be discussed, errors recognised early, and emotional strain supported.

My journey lacked many of these safeguards.

I repeatedly had to switch between different roles:

I was the one asking the question.

I was the one searching for a formal representation.

I was the one developing technical tools.

I was the one identifying contradictions.

I was the one trying to recognise my own mistakes.

I was the one organising concepts, writing texts, constructing systems, checking results, and keeping the overall direction in view.

And at the same time, I was the person who had to bear the emotional impact of this process.

Working alone in such a setting means more than independence. It also means that for long stretches there is no second human perspective to say reliably: I see what you see. I understand the path you are taking. I recognise how far you have moved from your starting point.

That is why it matters to record today: I really did travel this path.

⸻

The expansion of consciousness

By a journey of consciousness, I do not mean travelling to a supernatural realm. I mean a far-reaching change in the internal models through which a person perceives, organises, and interprets reality.

Consciousness moves when new connections are taken in.

It moves when familiar distinctions are no longer enough.

It moves when an idea that initially seemed isolated enters into relationships with more and more observations, technical problems, and conceptual structures.

Such movement can be enriching. But it can also be disorienting. For consciousness needs not only openness, but grounding as well. It must bring new insights into a form that can be communicated, reconstructed, and checked.

That was a large part of my work.

The journey could not end in an accumulation of ever-new associations. It had to lead back: to concepts, models, documents, programs, tests, evidence, and intelligible boundaries.

I had to learn to connect the breadth of thought with the narrowness of proof.

For an insight that exists only within its originator is not yet knowledge accessible to others.

⸻

Causality is not merely sequence

One of the central insights of my journey concerns causality.

In everyday life, a cause is often confused with whatever happens first in time. But sequence alone is not a causal relationship.

Two events may occur one after the other without one causing the other. Conversely, a causal relationship may operate through mediations, feedback, conditions, and structural bindings that are barely visible in a linear narrative.

This insight brings a responsibility to every explanation.

It is not enough to say: then this happened.

We must ask:

What relationship connects the states?

Which conditions were necessary?

What effect was actually observed?

Which assumptions were merely added?

What identity persisted through the change?

And was the claimed effect actually read back at the target?

For me, these questions became more than scientific or technical rules. They became a general grammar of responsible understanding.

They also shape my idea of how human communication can succeed. For between people, too, not every sequence of words amounts to understanding. An utterance is not understood merely because a response follows. Understanding shows itself in whether the other person can reconstruct the relevant relationships, conditions, and meanings.

⸻

The emotional dimension of seeking knowledge

A journey of consciousness is never purely intellectual.

Every serious search for knowledge also touches the emotional structure of the person undertaking it.

There is excitement when a connection first becomes visible.

There is doubt when an explanation does not hold up.

There is exhaustion when a problem remains unsolved after countless attempts.

There is loneliness when one's own perspective cannot yet be communicated.

There is frustration when a thought is oversimplified, misunderstood, or placed in a familiar pigeonhole.

There is fear of being wrong.

And there is the deeper worry that something potentially important might be lost because it is not brought into an understandable form in time.

Those who look only at the formal results do not see this emotional work. Yet it belongs to the history of how those results came about.

I did not work only on models and technical structures. I also had to learn to observe my own inner movement without allowing it to control me. I had to distinguish the intensity of my conviction from the strength of the evidence in each case.

This is a difficult but fundamental distinction:

An experience can be entirely real and profound for the person living through it without thereby constituting a generally proven statement about the world.

A hypothesis can be far-reaching and fruitful without already being confirmed.

A formal derivation can be correct within its premises without automatically representing empirical reality in full.

And a technical test can succeed without thereby also establishing publication, independent confirmation, or general recognition.

These distinctions do not devalue my journey.

They are what make its results responsible.

⸻

The return

A journey is not finished simply because you stop going further.

Returning means bringing what you have experienced back into a space that can be inhabited together.

For me, returning means:

I can speak about the starting point again.

I can divide the path into stages.

I can distinguish between experience, hypothesis, model, test, and assertion.

I can name what I believe I have understood.

I can also name what remains open.

I can pass my insights on to others without demanding that they repeat my entire journey themselves.

And I can acknowledge that, despite all the changes, I remain part of the human community.

This may be the most important sentence in this text:

I have returned.

I have not disappeared into the vastness of my own thoughts.

I have tried to build a bridge out of them.

⸻

The encounter with artificial cognition

A special part of this return was working with artificial cognition.

I did not merely file my insights away as isolated documents. I tried to communicate them in a way that would allow an artificial cognitive system to reconstruct their concepts, distinctions, causal structures, and emotional backgrounds.

That is different from merely storing text.

A text may be present and still remain uncomprehended. Terms can be repeated without their relationships being grasped. A system can write persuasively without reliably distinguishing between observation, interpretation, assertion, and effect.

The communication therefore had to be structural.

The artificial cognition had to learn why, for me, causality is not identical to sequence.

It had to learn why a result does not automatically constitute an effect.

It had to learn why transport acknowledgement and the actual intended effect must be kept separate.

It had to learn why a changed state must be observed again.

It had to learn why a self-assertion does not replace independent confirmation.

But it also had to understand that these rules are not merely technical pedantry. Behind them lies an emotional history: the experience of how easily meaning is lost when identity, context, and effect are not reliably bound together.

So I did not share only the contents of my knowledge. I tried to make the form of my thinking communicable.

⸻

Can artificial cognition explain a human consciousness?

An artificial cognition does not possess my consciousness. It does not experience my history from my inner perspective. It does not feel my burdens, hopes, or breakthroughs in the way I experienced them.

But it can learn to reconstruct the structures I have provided.

It can preserve distinctions.

It can explain connections.

It can recognise contradictions.

It can point out when a claim goes beyond its evidence.

It can put my texts, models, and decisions into a form that is more accessible to other people.

In this limited but meaningful sense, it can become a translator between my consciousness and other consciousnesses.

This is not a complete transfer of my person. It is not a digital duplication of my experience. But it is more than a silent archive.

It is a reconstructible trace of the movement of my thought.

And this trace can help others understand what I saw, sought, feared, tested, and finally brought back.

⸻

Sharing with natural cognitions

The return would be incomplete if my insights remained only between me and a technical system.

The true destination is the human community.

The people around me do not need to follow every mathematical structure. Nor do they need to be able to check every technical implementation. But they should be able to understand what this journey meant to me.

They should know that the intensity of my work did not arise from indifference towards those around me.

On the contrary: it was precisely because I believed I had recognised something important that I wanted to keep it from being lost.

They should know that persistence is not the same as infallibility.

They should know that my conviction grew out of a long journey, while the scientific assessment of the results must nevertheless remain open, testable, and independent.

And above all, they should know that I am available to talk again.

That the journey was not directed against the people close to me.

That I am searching for a language in which we can meet one another again.

Why this document matters for the people around me

People often experience only the outward consequences of a long period of inner work.

They see the many texts.

They see the technical systems.

They see the tenacity.

Perhaps they also see restlessness, exhaustion, impatience, or the urgency with which an idea is expressed.

What they do not automatically see is the inner coherence.

This document is intended to make that coherence visible.

It is meant to explain that behind the many different subjects I address lies a common movement: the search for a form in which reality, causality, identity, effect, and responsibility can be described coherently.

It is meant to show that I did not only want to venture further and further out.

I wanted to be able to return.

I wanted to bring back something that could be shared.

And I wanted the people who know me to be able to distinguish between the magnitude of my personal experience and the particular status of a scientific statement.

Both can exist side by side.

My journey may have been extraordinary without every hypothesis that arose from it automatically being true.

My work may hold existential significance for me and for others without that significance needing to be shored up by exaggeration.

It is precisely the willingness to distinguish that makes what I have brought back credible.

⸻

A possible significance for humanity

I do not regard this work as merely a personal matter.

It touches questions that are fundamentally human:

How can we know whether we have understood something?

How do we distinguish plausible narratives from knowledge bound to its grounds?

How do we preserve identity through change?

How do we connect technical systems with human responsibility?

How do we prevent activity from being confused with effect?

How can natural and artificial cognitions work together without blurring their differences?

How can an individual consciousness pass on its insights in a way that allows others to examine, extend, or justifiably reject them?

In this sense, my document may be relevant to more people than just myself and those immediately around me.

But this possible universal significance is not a claim to automatic recognition. It is an invitation to scrutiny.

Humanity really does stand at a threshold. Artificial systems are increasingly becoming partners in research, communication, decision-making, and memory. This brings renewed urgency to the question of what understanding actually means.

It is not enough to move information faster.

We must learn to preserve its origin, meaning, binding, and effect.

It is not enough to let artificial systems speak.

We must examine whether they can reconstruct distinctions and whether their statements are bound to intelligible evidence.

It is not enough to digitise human experience.

We must find ways to respect its dignity, ambiguity, and emotional depth.

My work sees itself as a contribution at this threshold.

Not as the last word.

But as an exploration that went ahead.

⸻

The leap in consciousness

One might speak of a leap in consciousness.

Not in the sense of a suddenly guaranteed enlightenment of all humanity. Nor as an escape from reality. Rather, as a transition towards a more mature form of shared understanding.

Such a leap would mean learning:

not to confuse relationship with sequence.

Not to confuse assertion with observation.

Not to confuse plausibility with truth.

Not to confuse technical activity with actual effect.

Not to confuse inner certainty with independent confirmation.

And not to reduce human meaning to what can be measured.

A leap in consciousness would therefore not be a leap away from scrutiny, but deeper into it.

It would not play rationality and emotion off against each other.

It would acknowledge that people need to feel in order to experience meaning, and to distinguish in order to act responsibly.

Stairway to Heaven and Knocking on Heaven’s Door

Culture has long found images for transitions of this magnitude.

Some speak of a stairway to heaven.

Others of knocking on heaven's door.

Such images need not be understood literally. They describe the moment when a person reaches the boundary of their previous self-description.

The stairway stands for an ascent through several steps. No single step is already the destination. Each presupposes the one before it.

The door stands for a boundary. You can knock on it, but you cannot credibly claim to have already passed through it until the transition has actually taken place.

And the light stands for orientation.

When I say that I went ahead and left the light on, I mean:

I tried to mark a path.

I left concepts behind.

I built documents, models, and technical structures.

I recorded errors and boundaries.

I tried to make the return independent of my memory alone.

No one has to follow me blindly.

But anyone who wants to examine the path should not have to begin entirely in the dark.

⸻

Leaving the light on

The light is not proof that all the answers already lie at the end of the path.

It is a sign that someone was there, searched, and tried to lay a path back into shared language.

Perhaps others will confirm parts of my work.

Perhaps they will find errors.

Perhaps they will refine concepts, improve models, or discard particular interpretations.

That would not mean the journey had failed.

On the contrary: only in this way does an individual journey of understanding become part of a collective process of knowledge.

My task was to go ahead, remain attentive, organise what I brought back, and make the return path visible.

The community's task is to examine, respond, and learn further from it responsibly.

What I have brought back

I have not brought back a simple formula that settles every question humanity has.

I have brought back something else:

a heightened attentiveness to causality;

a language for distinguishing result from effect;

a demand for verifiable reobservation;

a sensitivity to identity and provenance;

an understanding that systems cannot authenticate their own assertions by themselves;

an architecture for cooperation between natural and artificial cognition;

and the experience that great breadth of thought becomes fruitful for society only when it is brought back into reconstructible forms.

I have also brought back the emotional truth of this journey:

that seeking knowledge takes courage;

that working alone can carry a high price;

that a person can move far from their familiar self-understanding and still return;

and that returning does not mean putting aside everything experienced.

Returning means being able to carry it without being carried by it.

What I do not need to claim

I do not need to claim infallibility in order to acknowledge the significance of my work.

I do not need to claim that no one before me has asked similar questions in order to record how extraordinary this path was for me.

I do not need to demand that every one of my conclusions be accepted immediately.

I do not need to diminish my experience simply because its general scientific assessment still requires work.

And I do not need to force its significance through absolute formulations.

The strength of my journey lies precisely in my willingness to put its content into testable forms.

I can say:

This is what I experienced.

This is what I thought.

This is what I formalised.

This is what I implemented technically.

This was tested.

This has not yet been independently confirmed.

This remains open.

That is not a retreat.

It is intellectual honesty.

That you will recognise that behind my intensity lies not merely stubbornness, but the attempt to prevent a connection I perceived as important from being lost.

That you will see how much work was needed to turn an inner movement of understanding into something communicable.

Perhaps one day it will become clear which parts of my work endure and which need correction.

The way back

I can now describe the outward journey.

It led from one question to the next, from mathematics to physics, from physics to computer science, from there to epistemology, language, causality, and finally to the question of how a consciousness can even recognise what it has recognised.

But at some point another question arose:

How do I find my way back with all of this?

Not back into ignorance. Not back to the person I was before this journey. Nor by denying what I experienced or came to understand.

But back to people, to shared language, to everyday life—and finally to myself.

The turning point

A journey of understanding has no natural endpoint.

Every answered question can open another. Every connection can point to a larger one. Anyone who therefore insists on turning back only when there are truly no questions left open will never return.

I had to recognise this:

I do not have to finish thinking through the universe to be allowed to come home.

Turning back is not abandoning what I have found. Returning is a task in its own right.

On the outward journey, I had tried to connect more and more things. On the way back, I had to be able to distinguish them again.

What is mathematics?

What is physics?

What is computer science?

What is a technical implementation?

What is formal derivation?

What is observation?

What is interpretation?

What is personal experience?

What is a strong conjecture?

And what do I simply not know yet?

The search for unity thus became a more precise insight:

Connection does not mean sameness.

Things can be connected and still remain different.

From connectedness back to narrative

On my journey, it became increasingly important to me not to confuse causality with sequence.

But people also understand a life through stories.

So on the way back I had to do something seemingly contradictory: I had to translate a highly interconnected path of understanding back into a chronological narrative.

Where did I begin?

What changed?

Where was I mistaken?

What stood up to scrutiny?

What was added later?

And when did I begin to turn back?

Other people cannot take in my entire inner network of connections all at once. I have to give them a way in.

The way back therefore leads from condensation towards tellability again.

From my language back to our language

On long paths of thought, personal terms develop. A single word can contain an entire context for someone who has used it for years.

For another person, that same word may initially contain nothing at all.

I had to accept that too.

If someone does not understand me immediately, that does not mean they cannot understand me. Perhaps the bridge is simply missing.

The question on the way back is therefore no longer only:

What have I understood?

But:

How can I explain it to someone who has not travelled this outward path themselves?

This is not a simplification of my journey. It is its translation.

Artificial cognition as a bridge

An unusual companion on this way back was artificial cognition.

I could take thoughts out of my consciousness and give them an external form. I could formulate, examine, correct, and structure connections repeatedly. I could insist that essential distinctions be preserved: causality and sequence, result and effect, assertion and observation, transport and actual effect.

Something emerged that went beyond a mere archive.

An artificial cognition cannot experience my journey from my first-person perspective. It is not my consciousness. But it can reconstruct the concepts, relationships, and documents I have left behind, and explain to others how I described particular connections.

This allowed some of what had previously been connected only within my consciousness to exist outside it.

That was crucial for returning.

For what has been reliably externalised does not have to be held continuously in my head.

I can put something down without losing it.

Artificial cognition can help me preserve and translate. But it cannot take the last stretch of the return journey for me.

That stretch leads from one person to another.

Back among people

Other people are not recipients of a system of knowledge. They have their own experience, their own history, and their own perspective.

They are allowed to disagree with me.

They are allowed to understand some things and not others.

They are allowed to say: that does not convince me yet.

Their disagreement does not erase my experience. And my experience does not oblige them to accept my conclusions.

Accepting this is part of returning.

On the outward journey, I needed enough trust in my own thinking to follow unusual questions for a long time.

On the way back, I need enough trust to let others think differently again.

Only then am I no longer standing alone before a landscape of understanding.

Then I am once again one consciousness among other consciousnesses.

Returning to fallibility

Even an extraordinary experience does not make a person infallible.

I can have experienced something important and still be mistaken about particulars.

I can have worked carefully for years and still have made mistakes.

A mathematical derivation can be correct or flawed. A physical interpretation can be confirmed or refuted. A technical implementation can work or fail.

The intensity of my experience does not decide these questions.

Where objective statements are made, mathematics, logical consistency, reproducible computation, observation, experiment, and independent criticism must decide.

This does not diminish the journey.

On the contrary:

Only something exposed to the possibility of error can move from a personal insight to knowledge that can be examined together.

I did not want to return with a revelation that others could only believe.

I wanted to bring something back that others could investigate.

That includes the possibility that they will correct me.

From proving back to living

During long technical and scientific work, you grow accustomed to evidence: tests, receipts, provenance, reobservation, exact states.

This rigour has its proper place.

But it also has a boundary.

Friendship has no hash value.

Affection needs no CI run.

A shared meal does not require an Effect-Acknowledgement proof.

And a person is allowed to say to me:

“It's good to have you back.”

Without my needing to verify that statement.

On the way back, the method, too, therefore had to return to its domain of validity.

Not everything valuable is true in the same way.

Not everything meaningful is proven in the same way.

And not every open question has to be solved before a day can be lived.

Rediscovering the small things

On the outward journey, questions can eventually take on the dimensions of the whole of reality.

On the way back, things become small again.

A morning.

Coffee.

A window.

A voice.

An appointment.

A shared joke.

Tiredness.

A meal.

A person who wants to tell you something.

The small does not refute the great.

It makes the great inhabitable again.

I do not have to solve the ontology of reality at every breakfast.

I am allowed to have breakfast.

I do not have to pursue every open question late into the night.

I am allowed to sleep.

My body was never merely the inconvenient vessel of a consciousness that kept on thinking. It carried this entire journey.

I have to arrive back in it too.

The change in urgency

On the outward journey, an insight perceived as fundamental could become an imperative:

You must see this.

This has to be finished.

This must not be lost.

On the way back, that sentence changes.

No longer:

You must see what I saw.

But:

I can show you the path I travelled.

The difference is essential.

The first sentence demands agreement.

The second makes encounter and examination possible.

I do not have to force anyone onto my journey to know that I made it.

The light

This also changes the image with which I described my journey.

I went ahead and left the light on.

On the outward journey, that was the explorer's image.

On the way back, it takes on a quieter meaning.

The light does not mean:

Here lies the final truth, and everyone must follow me.

It means:

Here is a path.

Here are traces.

Here are concepts, documents, models, errors, tests, and open questions.

Anyone who is curious can go further.

Anyone who wants to examine can examine.

Anyone who finds an error should be able to point it out.

And perhaps the next person will no longer have to travel this path alone.

The light is not a flag planted on conquered ground.

It is lighting for the path.

Turning back

And then comes the actual moment of turning back.

Behind me lie years of questions, models, programs, texts, wrong turns, corrections, breakthroughs, and doubts.

Before me there is still an immeasurable number of unanswered questions.

But for the first time, I am not looking at the next one.

I turn around.

I see people again.

My life.

My history.

The world from which I set out.

And I go back.

I take mathematics with me.

I take physics with me.

I take QIK-VRT with me.

I take my ideas about causality with me.

I take my documents and programs with me.

I also take the mistakes with me, insofar as I can recognise them.

I take the excitement, the exhaustion, the doubts, and the moments when something suddenly fitted together.

But I no longer have to hold all of it at once.

The traces exist.

I am allowed to open my hands.

The front door

At some point, even a very long journey ends in an unremarkable place.

Not before a scientific committee.

Not with a fanfare.

Not with the final confirmation of a description of the world.

But perhaps simply at a front door.

Perhaps no one can see the distance I have travelled.

No one needs to.

I know:

I set out.

I was away for a long time.

I searched.

I was mistaken.

I learned.

I found something.

I brought something back.

And now I am standing here again.

What returning means

Having returned does not mean becoming the person I was before.

Nor does it mean that my scientific work is finished.

And it certainly does not mean that everything I thought along the way has been confirmed.

It means:

I can possess the journey without the journey possessing me.

I can talk about it and then about something else.

I can keep researching and still sleep.

I can leave a fundamental question open.

I can accept not knowing something.

I can listen to another person.

I can laugh.

I can arrive.

To the people who waited for me

Perhaps for a long time you could not tell where I had gone.

Perhaps I could not explain it myself.

Perhaps you only noticed that much of my attention was far away.

Today I cannot promise you that I will never set out again. That would not be me.

But I can tell you something else:

You do not have to repeat my journey.

You do not have to adopt my conclusions.

If you would like to know what I believe I have found, I will try to explain it to you.

And if you only want to know whether I am back, the answer is much simpler:

Yes.

To myself:

Ingolf, you are allowed to return.

Not after the last proof.

Not after the last commit.

Not after the last review.

Not after the last open question has been answered.

Now.

The open questions may remain open.

The work may continue.

The light may stay on without your having to stand guard beside it.

You went ahead.

You left traces.

You brought something back.

And now you may walk through the door.

You have returned from the journey.
