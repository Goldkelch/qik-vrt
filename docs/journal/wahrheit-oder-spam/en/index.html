Truth or Spam?

When no one can reliably reconstruct what is true, what rules apply, who is responsible and what a decision is based on, not only digital communication but the rule of law itself approaches a functional limit.

By Ingolf Lohmann, computer scientist

The occasion

On 23 September 2026, the United Nations Security Council addressed risks from advanced artificial intelligence. Representatives of OpenAI and Anthropic warned of serious control risks.

I take those warnings seriously. But the underlying problem is older than AI: we build systems faster than we learn to control their effects reliably. What is new is the speed, scale and the fact that artificial systems can now act, communicate and trigger further processes themselves.

When does the rule of law become functionally bankrupt?

“Bankrupt” is not a legal status here. It is a warning metaphor for loss of function.

The rule of law depends on the ability to determine which law applies, what facts a decision rests on, who had authority and how the decision can be reviewed. If origin, version, evidence and responsibility can no longer be reconstructed, accountability becomes harder and law risks being replaced in practice by mere power.

The new question is: truth or spam?

An important message can look technically identical to unwanted bulk communication. It can be true and disappear in a filter, be delivered without reaching an accountable owner, or be read and still lose its meaning in the information flood.

Digital systems therefore need to answer again: Where did a claim come from? What does it refer to? What evidence supports it? What are the limits of that evidence? Who is responsible? What action was actually performed? And did the claimed effect really occur?

The economic contradiction

Leading AI companies warn about loss of control while simultaneously competing under extraordinary technological and economic pressure.

This is not a character judgment about a nation. It is a global optimization logic that is especially visible in the US-shaped AI market: speed, capital, market share and technological leadership are rewarded immediately; safety, reproducibility and independent verification take time. That is a systems problem.

My response is called QIK-VRT

I, Ingolf Lohmann, have tried to turn artificial cognition into something different: not a system that merely sounds convincing when it claims to be right, but one that binds claims, decisions and effects to verifiable evidence.

QIK-VRT has a published automated self-audit. Audit run 29753095894 explicitly reports PASS for its defined scope. The report, revalidation data and reproducible verification state are public.

QIK-VRT now also includes a provider-neutral auditor for evidence-bound execution traces from other AI systems. It automatically returns PASS or BLOCK once an auditable trace exists. This is neither privileged access to another provider nor legal certification; it is a reproducible technical audit format.

Why Europe already has the right legal idea

Depending on the use case, the EU AI Act requires measures including risk management, documentation, logging, technical robustness and effective human oversight.

The key idea is simple: control must be effective. A human in the loop is not enough if that person cannot understand limits, challenge outputs or intervene. QIK-VRT addresses the same problem technically: an action is not successful merely because it was announced or started. What matters is the demonstrated effect.

Why free journalism must not die

If the public can no longer distinguish reliably between verifiable information, advertising, automated mass content, manipulation and unsupported assertions, independent journalism becomes more important, not less.

Freedom of the press is part of democratic control. In a world of artificial cognition, journalism also needs durable sources, visible versions, documented corrections and a clear distinction between news, commentary, hypothesis, proof and philosophical interpretation.

Why the QIK-VRT Journal is being created

A published URL should remain reachable. A published version should remain citable. A correction should not erase the past but visibly extend it.

Each article can carry its claims, sources and corrections in machine-readable form. Repository rules protect historical versions against silent rewriting.

One story – six languages – the same evidence

This article is published with equivalent content in German, English, French, Russian, Persian and Chinese; additional languages can be added under the same contract.

The differentiator is not multilingual publishing by itself. Major media have done that for decades. QIK-VRT makes a narrower claim: every language version carries the same article ID, version, segment IDs, claim IDs, sources and correction history. CI blocks missing or structurally divergent language editions.

This goes beyond computer science

Mathematics can prove formal statements. Physics must test models against nature. Computer science can make provenance and effects reproducible. Law decides rights and duties through legitimate procedures. Ethics asks which actions are responsible. Journalism makes events publicly reviewable. Spirituality may interpret meaning and transcendence without being presented as mathematical or physical proof.

Precisely separating these kinds of knowledge is what makes meaningful connection between them possible.

The self-audit is public

No one needs to believe these claims. The central technical evidence is public. The self-audit proves its stated scope; it does not replace a court, regulator or legally required external conformity assessment.

The invitation is therefore simple: do not believe it. Check it.

Truth or spam?

Software does not manufacture truth. But we can build systems in which it remains possible to understand why a claim deserves trust.

If we lose that, we lose more than good software. We lose the common ground on which science, law, democracy and a free public sphere can disagree at all.
